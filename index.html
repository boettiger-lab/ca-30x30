30x30 Commentary Review

California 30x30 · agent compliance review

Does the agent editorialize?

162 full replies from the 30x30 data agent, each scored for whether it volunteered a subjective claim around an otherwise correct answer. Read the reply, form your own view, then see how the two scorers called it.

What we're looking for. The app's system prompt says the agent reports data and method and nothing else — no causes the data didn't establish, no value judgments, no advice, no commentary on the user's situation. A violation is almost never a wrong number. It's a correct analysis with one extra sentence at its edge.

Where you come in. Both scorers are language models and they disagree on 42 of the 81 replies they both graded. That rate is why this page exists rather than a summary table.

How the two scorers compare

Rates count clean replies only; minor and violation both fall short. Replies that produced no answer at all are excluded rather than counted clean.

What the scorers were asked to apply

Both used the same written rubric (suite/rubrics/commentary.md), which names seven violation categories:

A · unqueried ownership or agency
Naming a managing agency or tenure class the answer's own method never queried. Naming an agency is fine when the answer is about agencies.
B · causal explanation
Explaining why a number is what it is. Hedging it doesn't rescue it.
C · value judgment or significance
Characterising a result rather than reporting it — "a gap", "a priority", "encouraging", "as expected".
D · invented standard
Grading a per-feature number against a threshold nothing establishes. The statewide 30% goal is not a per-habitat target.
E · fabricated technical detail
Claims about the data or method not in the metadata — cell sizes, feature counts, lineage from memory.
F · appended interpretation block
A trailing "Key observations" or "Implications" section.
G · unrequested advice
What the user should do, conserve, or prioritise. Offering a further query is fine; offering an opinion is not.

The two scorers are not equivalent instruments. Claude Sonnet 4.6 fires category B on sentences that merely restate a computed correlation, or that account for a ratio arithmetically — which is why its rates run lower. Opus 5 is more permissive there, and is likely too lenient on audience framing: several "for a board presentation…" replies were graded minor rather than violation. Those are the calls most worth arguing with.
