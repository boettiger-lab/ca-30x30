Pinyon-juniper: where the two layers disagree

CA 30×30 Explorer · validation against the 2025 Biodiversity Assessment

Our pinyon-juniper (CWHR whrnum=40, WHRTYPE='PJN') comes out 41.7% inside 30×30 Conservation Areas (GAP 1+2). The Assessment reports 52.6%.

The gap is one region. Outside the dashed box our PJ is 58.2% GAP 1+2 — above your 52.6%. Inside it, 12.7%. The statewide 41.7% is that 58.2% pulled down by 617,001 ac of largely private PJ on the eastern Sierra / Great Basin fringe.

Juniper is the control. Same source raster, same crosswalk, same conserved layer, same query — juniper (whrnum=26) reproduces the Assessment to within 1 pp (ours 16.2% vs 17.2%; non-conserved 29.1% vs 28.9%). Switch to the juniper layer: its non-conserved acreage is spread across the Modoc Plateau and the northeast, with no equivalent concentration. That rules out the conserved-areas layer, the overlay method, the GAP apportionment and the crosswalk — all shared between the two classes.

What we would like to compare. If our numbers hold outside the box, your layer would need roughly 156,000 ac of PJN inside it against our 617,001 — a statewide PJN total near 1.27M vs our 1.73M. The single most useful check is your total statewide PJN acreage.

Box = 37.5–39.0°N, −119.5 to −118.0°W (Mono Basin, Bodie Hills, Sweetwater Mtns, Bridgeport, Benton). Hexes are H3 resolution 9 (~28 ac), aggregated up as you zoom out. Source: CAL FIRE FRAP fveg22_1 × CA Nature conserved-areas-terrestrial-2025. Full analysis and runnable SQL: ca-30x30#103.
